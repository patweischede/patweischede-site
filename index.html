How I read it

The constraint wasn't conversion. It was the show rate.

Most people would've chased more reach. But a 10-point lift on a 24.4% show rate is ~170 more people in the room, which at 10.4% conversion is roughly 40% more paid clients for zero extra spend. So the next campaign targets the gap between "registered" and "showed up": reminder sequencing and a small commitment at sign-up.

Data honesty

The platform exports said 1,756 registrations and 423 attendees. Deduplicated, the true numbers were 1,690 and 413, a 3.8% overstatement. If you're making offer decisions on inflated dashboards, you're making them wrong. I fix the numbers before I touch the funnel.

How I think about offers

One audience. Four temperatures. One job per offer.

Cannibalisation happens when two offers do the same job for the same person. The fix isn't fewer offers. It's giving every offer exactly one job, one audience temperature and one next step.

Add an offer when…

  1. The same question keeps coming up that no current offer answers.
  2. There's a big drop-off between two steps that a smaller commitment could bridge.
  3. Your core offer is at capacity and buyers are waiting.

Cut or merge when…

  1. Two offers answer the same question for the same buyer.
  2. It sells, but it leads nowhere. A dead end, not a step.
  3. You can't say in one sentence who it's not for.

Fix it, don't add, when…

  1. The offer is right but the show rate or follow-up is broken.
  2. Leads are going cold before anyone talks to them.
  3. The numbers you're deciding on haven't been cleaned.

Questions

Fair questions.

Your results are in immigration. Does that apply to my business?

The niche is specific, the mechanics aren't. Any business where the buyer has to trust you before paying (coaching, advisory, consulting, professional services) runs on the same engine: reach new people, earn a small yes, deliver, retain. Immigration is just a particularly unforgiving place to learn it.

Do I need a big audience to start?

No. Plenty of businesses I'd work with have more leads than they're converting. If you're getting enquiries and losing them, we start with the catching, not the reach.

What does "AI in lead gen" actually mean here?

Practical, boring, useful things: scoring and routing enquiries on arrival, drafting follow-ups and client documents, summarising calls into your CRM. Always with a human checkpoint where judgement matters. No chatbots pretending to be you.

Will you build it, or just tell me what to do?

Either. Some clients want the audit and a plan for their team. Others want me to build the pipeline with them. We'll work out which fits on the first call.
