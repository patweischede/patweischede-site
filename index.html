Work

Six things I built

01 · Marketing

Offshore client acquisition campaign

In-market seminars are the practice's main acquisition channel for a market 8,000 km away, delivered by a team in a different country and time zone. Six sessions across three cities.

Built: the acquisition stack end to end. Registration forms, email campaigns, the QR-to-payment funnel, booking, venue coordination and the seminar decks. Organic-first rather than buying reach, on the basis that the practice's content already had distribution. A four-person offshore team executed on a weekly cadence while I held the design.

Data integrity finding Raw platform exports reported 1,756 registrations and 423 attendees. Deduplicated against source data the true figures were 1,690 and 413: a 3.8% overstatement. I rebuilt campaign reporting on the deduplicated base, which gave the practice a defensible funnel benchmark for the first time.
How I read the result The show rate is the constraint, not the conversion rate. A ten-point lift on 24.4% is roughly 170 additional attendees, which at 10.4% conversion is roughly 40% more output for no additional spend. The next run targets the registration-to-attendance gap with reminder sequencing and a commitment device at registration.

02 · Funnel

End-to-end consultation pipeline

Moving a client from enquiry to a paid consultation involved a manual handoff at every step. Clients who had already paid waited days for confirmation and booking instructions.

Built: an automated path from landing page to payment link to booking to video call to post-consultation sequence, with no manual handoff at any point. Conditional transactional email segments each enquiry on submission and routes it to a sequence matched to intent. Two payment rails, card and local wallet, feed the same confirmation flow, so the offshore market runs on the same automation as the domestic one.

03 · Systems

The practice's first CRM

Client tracking lived in spreadsheets on a single local machine. No shared source of truth, no pipeline visibility, no continuity if that machine or that person was unavailable, for a caseload spanning two countries.

Built: a stage-gated CRM in Notion as one linked structure rather than a set of separate trackers. Seven pipeline stages from enquiry to closed engagement, four linked databases (client CRM, team activity, HR hub, weekly schedule), three months from first design to team-adopted MVP.

What I got wrong The build was not the hard part. Adoption was. I had designed for the system rather than for the people using it, and rollout stalled because the team had to learn a new tool on top of doing their jobs. The fix was automating the entries so the system filled itself. Tool selection is not a technical decision: it is a function of who is on the team and whether the change actually benefits them. I assess that before I build now, not after.

04 · AI

AI-assisted document generation pipeline

The flagship client deliverable is a structured multi-section assessment report. It took three hours per report, produced entirely by hand, and it was the binding constraint on how many clients could be served.

Built: a generation pipeline on a structured retrieval knowledge base, tuned over six months before it was scaled into use. Pre-consultation intake forms and meeting transcription wired in as inputs, so it starts from captured data rather than notes. A human review gate at the end: the pipeline drafts, it does not file.

The judgement behind it The six months of tuning is the point, not the speed. In a regulated context the failure mode of a fast wrong answer is worse than the cost of a slow right one, so the effort went into retrieval quality rather than throughput, and the review step stayed human.
